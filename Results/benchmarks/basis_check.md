# Basis check: DFT training labels vs experimental benchmark labels

GENERATED by `scripts/basis_check.py`. Do not hand-edit.

The models are trained on Materials Project DFT-relaxed lattice parameters.
The benchmark `a_true` columns are experimental. These are different quantities,
so part of every benchmark error is basis mismatch, not model error.

MP's `theoretical: False` means the structure has been observed, not that the
lattice parameters were measured. All MP lattice parameters are computed.

### Why no correction factor is shipped

1. n = 3. Three anchors cannot define a correction over a 64,128-row training domain.
2. The sign flips: UN −0.006621, UC −0.022736, CeO2 +0.057365. A single global offset is
   contradicted by these three points, so their mean is not computed.
3. The delta is not attributable. It bundles exchange-correlation functional error, 0 K
   (DFT) against finite-temperature (experiment) thermal expansion, zero-point motion,
   and error in the experimental value itself. Three numbers cannot separate four causes.

### The temperature confound

DFT relaxes at 0 K, without zero-point motion. The experimental values are measured at some
T > 0, and for UN at a temperature the source does not state (COD 9008757, Wyckoff
*Crystal Structures* 1963, carries no `_cell_measurement_temperature`). Every material
expands on heating, so `a_exp(T) > a_exp(0 K)`, and:

```
DFT − a_exp(0K)  =  [DFT − a_exp(T)]  +  [a_exp(T) − a_exp(0K)]
                 =   measured delta   +   a strictly positive term
```

The same-temperature delta is therefore more positive than the measured delta, which
determines which of the three anchors can be relied on:

| anchor | measured delta | same-temperature delta | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| CeO2 | +0.057365 | more positive still | holds; DFT exceeds experiment |
| UN | −0.006621 | less negative, possibly positive | sign undetermined |
| UC | −0.022736 | less negative, possibly positive | sign undetermined |

A thermal-expansion term on a ~4.9 Å cell over a few hundred K is plausibly the same order
as the UN and UC deltas. The CeO2 result therefore stands, and with it the explanation for
the uniform over-prediction on (Ce,Nd)O2, but the claim that DFT under-estimates UN and UC
does not follow. That would require a cited thermal-expansion coefficient, which this
repository does not hold. Only the sign of the correction is known, not its magnitude.

### What a calibration reference would need

Per entry, checked against a candidate (COD 9008757, UN), which fails 3 of 6:

| # | Requirement | COD 9008757 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `_cell_measurement_temperature`, mandatory | absent |
| 2 | standard uncertainty on `_cell_length_a` (`4.884(2)`) | bare `4.884` |
| 3 | measured stoichiometry (UN₁₋ₓ / UO₂₊ₓ, since `a` depends strongly on O/M) | just "UN" |
| 4 | primary diffraction source, not a compilation | Wyckoff 1963 textbook tabulation |
| 5 | spacegroup matches the MP entry | yes, Fm-3m (225) = mp-1865 |
| 6 | conventional-cell setting | yes, Z=4, a = 4.884 Å |

`_cell_length_a` = 4.884 Å in that CIF is `UNUC.csv`'s `a_true` for `U1 N1` exactly, so the
benchmark is likely built on that same 1963 value and inherits its missing temperature and
missing uncertainty. It is not an independent check on this problem.

Per set: N large enough to fit and hold out, dozens rather than 3; spanning the training
domain rather than fuels alone; every entry matched to a specific MP `material_id` with the
same spacegroup and composition; the calibration expressed as a learned map (DFT a → exp a
at a reference T, with structure and composition features) rather than a single scalar,
which the sign flip across these three points already rules out; and held-out validation
reporting residual and CI. ICSD records temperature more consistently than COD and would be
the better source.

## (B) Basis-invariant model metrics

A constant offset cannot change the slope or the correlation of predicted against true, so
these measure what each model learned about composition and stay valid whatever the
DFT/experiment delta turns out to be.

`bias` is the mean signed error (the offset). `scatter` is the std of the error after
removing that bias, i.e. how well the model tracks the trend once the offset is gone.

### Reading these

- MAE vs exp is not pure model error, because it contains the basis mismatch. Do not quote
  it as "the model is wrong by X Å" without saying what it is compared against.
- Slope and Pearson r are the comparison to use. A model with a large bias but slope ≈ 1
  and r ≈ 1 has learned the composition dependence and is offset. A model with small bias
  but r ≈ 0 has learned nothing and lands near the right answer by coincidence.

Independence caveat: `Data/README.md` records that 19 of UNUC.csv's 23 rows are sampled
from the quadratic fit to the literature values, so `a_true` there is largely a fitted
curve rather than 23 independent measurements, and correlation against it measures
fidelity to that fit. CeO2Nd2O3Vals.csv has n = 7, so its slope carries real
uncertainty. Weigh both by their n.
